# Ingredient care and using things up

## Safety rule

If it smells bad, is slimy, mouldy or tastes bitter, do not cook it. Throw it away and tell Madam/Sir. When in doubt, do not use it.

## Fresh items

- **Tomato**: Storage: At room temperature away from sun; in the fridge if very ripe. Lasts: 3-5 days. Signs it has gone off: Mould, leaking juice, sour smell. Throw away. If old: Puree over-ripe tomatoes and add to dal or gravy.
- **Onion**: Storage: In a dry, airy place; not next to potatoes. Lasts: 2-3 weeks. Signs it has gone off: Wet, very soft, black mould or smell. Cut away and discard the rotten part. If old: A little sprouting is fine if the onion is firm and does not smell.
- **Potato**: Storage: In a dark, cool, dry place; away from onions. Lasts: 2-3 weeks. Signs it has gone off: Green colour, heavy sprouting, soft or rotten. If old: Do not eat green parts or heavily sprouted potatoes (can be harmful); cut light sprouts out completely.
- **Spinach**: Storage: Unwashed, wrapped in paper, in the fridge. Lasts: 2-3 days. Signs it has gone off: Yellowing, sliminess, smell. Throw away. If old: If wilted but not slimy, blanch and cook it today.
- **Paneer**: Storage: In the fridge, submerged in water (change the water daily). Lasts: 3-5 days once opened, or until the pack date. Signs it has gone off: Sour smell, sliminess, yellow film. Throw away. If old: Paneer that has firmed up: soak in warm water for 10 minutes to soften; use in bhurji or gravy.
- **Cauliflower**: Storage: In the fridge, kept dry. Lasts: 4-5 days. Signs it has gone off: Spreading black spots, sliminess, smell. If old: Cut off small dark spots; stalks and soft leaves can go in the sabzi too.
- **Peas**: Storage: In the fridge; in the freezer if you have a lot. Lasts: 5-6 days fresh, months frozen. Signs it has gone off: Sliminess, smell, mould. If old: If you have a lot, boil and freeze.
- **Atta**: Storage: In an airtight container, in a cool dry place. Lasts: 2-3 months. Signs it has gone off: Insects or webbing, damp, or an odd smell. If so, throw away that part and tell Madam/Sir.
- **Curd**: Storage: In the fridge. Lasts: 5-7 days. Signs it has gone off: Mould, pink or green film, bitter smell. Throw away. If old: If sour but not mouldy, use in kadhi, marinades or paratha dough.
- **Cream**: Storage: In the fridge. Lasts: 3-4 days once opened. Signs it has gone off: Sour smell, curdled. Throw away. If old: Use a small amount in palak or paneer gravy.
- **Cucumber**: Storage: In the fridge. Lasts: 4-5 days. Signs it has gone off: Very soft, slimy, bitter. Throw away if bitter. If old: If soft, remove the seeds and use in raita.
- **Milk**: Storage: Boiled, in the fridge. Lasts: 1-2 days after boiling. Signs it has gone off: Curdling, sour smell. If old: Split milk can make home-made paneer, but throw it away if it smells.
- **Egg**: Storage: In the fridge. Lasts: 3-4 weeks. Signs it has gone off: Smell when cracked; floats in water if very old. If old: Cook old eggs thoroughly.
- **Chicken**: Storage: In the coldest part of the fridge, in a separate box. Lasts: 1-2 days. Signs it has gone off: Smell, sliminess, green-grey colour. If old: Do not use after the pack expiry date. Throw it away. Wash utensils and board used for raw chicken separately and cook it thoroughly.
- **Bhindi**: Storage: Dry, in cloth or paper; do not keep wet. Lasts: 2-3 days. Signs it has gone off: Sliminess, black spots, soft. If old: If it has gone soft, cook it today.
- **Brinjal**: Storage: In a cool place or the fridge. Lasts: 4-5 days. Signs it has gone off: Wrinkled, soft rotten spots. If old: Cut off small spots.
- **Cabbage**: Storage: In the fridge. Lasts: 1 week. Signs it has gone off: Rotten smell, slimy leaves. If old: Remove spoiled outer leaves.
- **Carrot**: Storage: In the fridge. Lasts: 1-2 weeks. Signs it has gone off: Mould, sliminess. If old: If rubbery, soak in cold water for 30 minutes to crisp up.
- **Beans**: Storage: In the fridge. Lasts: 3-4 days. Signs it has gone off: Sliminess, black spots. If old: Remove strings from older beans.
- **Capsicum**: Storage: In the fridge. Lasts: 5-6 days. Signs it has gone off: Very soft, black spots. If old: If wrinkled, cook today.
- **Lauki**: Storage: In the fridge. Lasts: 4-5 days. Signs it has gone off: Soft rotten spots, smell. If old: Taste a small piece first. If bitter, throw the whole thing away and do not cook it (bitter lauki can be harmful).
- **Mushroom**: Storage: In a paper bag in the fridge, unwashed. Lasts: 2-3 days. Signs it has gone off: Sliminess, black spots, smell. Throw away. If old: Cook quickly; do not eat slimy mushrooms.
- **Besan**: Storage: In an airtight container, in a cool dry place. Lasts: 2-3 months. Signs it has gone off: Insects or webbing, damp, or an odd smell. If so, throw away that part and tell Madam/Sir.
- **Suji**: Storage: In an airtight container, in a cool dry place. Lasts: 2-3 months. Signs it has gone off: Insects or webbing, damp, or an odd smell. If so, throw away that part and tell Madam/Sir.
- **Bread**: Storage: In a dry place, not the fridge. Lasts: 3-4 days. Signs it has gone off: Green or white mould. Check the whole pack and throw it away. If old: Day-old bread (no mould) makes toast, upma or bread pakora.
- **Kuttu**: Storage: In an airtight container, in a cool dry place. Lasts: 1-2 months. Signs it has gone off: Insects or webbing, damp, or an odd smell. If so, throw away that part and tell Madam/Sir.
- **Banana**: Storage: At room temperature. Lasts: 3-4 days. Signs it has gone off: Very black, leaking juice, smell. If old: Very ripe bananas: paratha, shake or halwa.
- **Apple**: Storage: In the fridge. Lasts: 1-2 weeks. Signs it has gone off: Soft rotten spots, mould. If old: Cut out soft spots.

## Dry items

- In airtight containers in a dry place. If there are insects, webbing, damp or a smell, throw away that part: dal, rice, atta, rajma, moong, masoor, chana, besan, poha, suji, sabudana, samak, peanuts

## Remember

What will spoil first today is told at the start of every call. This document only says how to handle each item.
